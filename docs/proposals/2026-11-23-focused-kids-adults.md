# Focused Kids / Adults approved course design — 2026-11-23

**L-012 · Course lists/design approved by Owner on2026-11-23 (“Yes it looks good”). No application or course-content change. Implementation plan/technical choices remain for review.**

## What the owner approved

- Add a Kids / Adults choice across the existing seven language directions. The approved Japanese direction remains English→Japanese only; no new language pairs.
- Start with approximately100–150 essential words and short expressions per course, preserving the existing510 outside these focused starting courses.
- Prioritize being understood and understanding a basic response. Common usable wording, familiar-word reuse and a small scope take precedence over formal polish or breadth. Politeness matters when misunderstanding or unsuitable use would result, rather than being the organizing objective.
- Draft the actual lists for review before changing the application. Implementation, translations, audio, merging and publication are not authorized by approval to draft.

## Approved shape

**100 items per course:80 shared +20 specific.** Ten proposed ten-item blocks each. There are120 distinct draft entries across both courses, not200 separately authored meanings. Start at100 to keep the promise of a small course; expand toward150 only for a demonstrably missing everyday need. This is a curated proposal, not a measured top100 ranking.

The first80 are deliberately identical in intended meaning across Kids and Adults and across source languages. The American and Russian learners would practise the same Japanese communicative intents. Target-language wording may need different grammar; it must express the same intent, not copy English word by word.

The blocks below organize content, not a new learning algorithm. Ten-consecutive-correct/third-mistake reset,500ms transition, audio replay/autoplay and ZERO saved learner state remain unchanged unless separately discussed. Kids/Adults and direction selections live only in current-session memory; no birth date, account or saved preference.

Kids means school-age situations here; no new age cutoff is asserted. Adults means general everyday situations, without specialist workplace vocabulary. The label controls content relevance, not “bad grammar for kids” versus formal speech for adults.

## How to read the lists

- `Existing vocab-NNNN`: exact normalized English label exists in the current course. Reuse is a candidate, not permission to copy an unresolved translation or distractor. Check the existing audit and pending correction PR #27.
- `Library cc-...`: wording already has a reference entry in the common-core library; its review status remains pending.
- `New draft`: no exact label match was found. Includes useful short patterns; it is not an allocated course ID, completed translation or finished recording. Related existing meanings may still be reusable after sense review.
- Ellipsis (`…`) means an editorial pattern slot, not an app text input or arbitrary sentence generator. Teach a small fixed example using taught words; do not assume slots can be filled identically in all languages. A pattern counts as one draft item; example expansions do not inflate the count.
- Questions and responses are both included. Name/origin examples use fictional people and places; the app must never collect personal learner information.

## Reuse and practice examples — not extra entries

- Shared: “I want water”; “Where is the toilet?” → “There”; “I don’t understand” → repeat or slower speech.
- Meeting: “What is your name?” → “My name is Ana”; “Where are you from?” → “I am from Latvia.”
- Kids: “Can I play?” → “Yes”; “It is your turn”; borrow a pen from a familiar classmate.
- Adults: “How much does this cost?” → a small number; “One ticket to [place], please”; asking whether a bus goes to that place.

Use a few taught nouns, places and numbers repeatedly. Do not add lists of foods, jobs, colors, animals, month names or greeting synonyms just to complete categories. The vocabulary focuses on a few useful conversations, not dictionary coverage. Kids play invitations refer to familiar peers; do not teach compliance with a stranger’s invitation.

## Language choices and unresolved work

- Japanese hello:こんにちは for daytime. Current public main still hasやあ; approved correction PR #27 is separate/unmerged. Matching per-symbol reading and audio must follow any eventual adoption. One greeting is enough initially; time-specific alternatives can remain outside the starter.
- Russian hello:здравствуйте already exists. Do not turn the draft into a choice among many politeness variants. Pick one understandable default for each situation, with a brief contextual note only when needed.
- Japanese often omits pronouns and distinguishes social/family context. Words such as mother/father need a clear intended use, rather than importing one isolated string into every situation. No universal literal “I need + noun” Japanese substitution is assumed.
- Food and thirst examples must use natural everyday expressions. The current Russian thirsty wording is an audit finding; do not blindly reuse it. Station, card and other broad words need their railway/payment sense pinned.
- Goodbye needs a practical departure scenario; do not silently treat Japaneseさようなら as the universal brief everyday farewell.
- Review each selected meaning and actual wrong answers in all seven directions. Duplicate surface forms are legitimate across different contexts, but must not compete as supposedly distinct answers in the same question.
- New/changed phrases need reviewed LV/RU/EN/JA text, matching audio and the authorized reading aids where applicable. The English labels above are intents, not approved translations. Reuse the library/audit sources and research only unresolved or new meanings.

## Design changes included in the approved proposal

1. Practical short phrases begin in lesson1, rather than waiting until lesson11. The current first-ten-block word/short-fragment restriction would otherwise prevent these focused courses from reaching their purpose.
2. Existing hard-coded agePath10–17 validation must support an explicit Kids/Adults course choice. Preserve IDs/content/assets; select small course manifests from the same canonical bank. Do not create two copies of the dictionary.
3. Review short-text limits by language and meaning instead of forcing a useful request into an unnatural four-word literal form. This is a proposal, not a removed validator.
4. Preserve all510 current entries outside the focused courses; their continued access/default presentation must be settled during UI design. Do not silently delete, rewrite or mix them back into a focused starting course.

## What would count as success

Using only the chosen core, learners should be able to attempt a short introduction, ask for water or a place, request repetition, state a simple preference, and obtain help. Kids should also join a familiar game or ask a school question; adults should handle a simple purchase or transport question. Evaluate listening to a basic response as well as recalling the phrase.

Multiple-choice completion alone cannot demonstrate spontaneous speaking or understanding natural-speed replies. Check these short situations with a fluent speaker before claiming the course achieves the communication goal. This proposal adds no microphone, chatbot, accounts, tracking or new exercise mode.
